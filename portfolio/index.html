05 How I work

Six habits, each from a real project

Ask before you build

Every sAIge client came with its own requirements. I designed the conversation flows around what they told me about their users and their systems, before anyone wrote code for them.

from sAIge

Fit into their stack, not ours

A new client isn't going to swap Okta for Azure AD to suit a vendor. So the bot changed instead: identity calls rewritten for Okta, tickets moved to Zendesk, and about 70% of that environment deployed by me.

from the password-reset replatform

Start from the person who's stuck

A password-reset bot gets used by someone locked out of their laptop, on a phone call, probably late for something. I designed the voice flows around that person: few steps, a one-time code instead of security questions, and a route to a human when the bot can't finish.

from sAIge and the password-reset bot

Measure what the customer is paying for

On the Okta and Zendesk rebuild, the client cared about one thing: how many resets the bot finished without a person. So the dashboard led with bot-versus-human resolution, with ticket counts at each stage underneath.

from the password-reset replatform

Write it down, then get it reviewed

Sixteen teams had to agree on how work moved between them. Every SOP went into markdown and every change went through a GitHub review, so each rule had an owner and a history. The content locked in three weeks.

from the campaign handbook

Make the rule visible

When I cut 1,463 influencers down to 273, every pick came with a written reason. Reviewers didn't have to trust a score. They could read the reason, disagree with a specific rule, and say so.

from the influencer shortlist
